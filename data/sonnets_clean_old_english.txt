1

When april waketh the hawthorn into bloom,
and mildest winds unbar the frozen field,
the lark ascends above the furrowed gloom,
while shadeth o' winter slowly quit and yield. 
so from mine own heart thy gentle presence driveth
the hoarded frost that long in silence lay,
and e'ry better hope within reviveth
to greet the promise o' thy temperate day. 
yet spring is frail, and clouds may dim the sun,
and tender petals fall ere they fruit;
still i would'st bless the good that hath begun,
though joy be brief and e'ry blossom mute. 
for one sweet hour, if all the year wert sere,
would'st teach the soul what maketh the living dear

2

The candle gutters in the scholar's room,
yet still his hand pursueth the patient line,
he wars withal time, withal weariness, withal gloom,
to snare in words some glimmer half divine. 
the midnight paneth return nay friendly face,
the house is hushed, the embers sink and sigh,
but thought keeps watch within that narrow place,
and feeds on stars that none but minds descry. 
so labor oft must bloom where none applaud,
and sooth be courted far from trumpet's breath;
its purchases are lean, its recompense seems flawed,
its bridegroom hours are kin to little death. 
yet what is won in silence, stern and slow,
may light an age long after we lie low.

3

When envy marks the wreath another wears,
and counts his honors withal a jaundiced eye,
it nurseth want and fattens private cares,
till peace grows thin and inward joys run dry. 
for others' praise can neither add nor take
the secret measure o' thy proper worth;
nay stolen star can make thy heaven shake,
nor borrowed dawn unmake thy native birth. 
turn then thy hand to that which thou canst tend,
and let thy craft, not coveting, be guide;
the fairest laurels from true labors bend,
and root more deep when vanity hath died. 
who blesseth others need not beg increase,
for generous hearts are wealthy most in peace.

4

At dusk the harbor gathers mast and sail,
and folds the sea's long glitter into gray,
the market clepes grow faint, the hammers fail,
and forweary men unyoke the toiling day. 
 one lamp by one begins along the quay,
like patient stars set base for mortal eyes,
while from the west the last reluctant ray
withdraws its gold and leaveth the cool surprise. 
 so after strife the spirit seeks a shore
where bruit falls off and troubled reckonings cease;
it asks not triumph, nor abundant store,
but one sure hearth and some sufficient peace. 
 the richest quietus is not in palaced sleep,
but where a faithful love its watch doth keep.

5

The oak wast once an acorn in the grass,
a slight green thought nay larger than a tear,
yet storms have bowed and many years did pass,
and still it stands, more rooted for the year. 
so virtue grows not by a single deed,
nor wisdom by one maxim apace learned;
they gather ring by ring from trial and need,
by losseth borne and errors slowly turned. 
mock not small acts because they seem too mean
to win the shout that follows grand display;
the brook begins where little springs are seen,
yet shapeth the stone and finds at last the bay. 
great souls are made by increments unseen,
and harvest ripens long ere the glean.

6

When summer fadeth beneath the amber sky,
and harvest bends the forweary fields withal gold,
the gluts wheel where cooling breezeth sigh,
and whisper taleth o' journeys yet untold. 
the orchard's fruit in quiet sweetness lies,
awaiting hands that pluck withal patient care,
while daylight dims in softly burning dyes,
and dusk lays purple fingers on the air. 
so too mine own youth, once ripe withal careless fire,
must yield its glow to time’s deliberate art,
yet memory guards each bright and bold desire,
and keeps their warmth alive within the heart. 
though seasons turn and fleeting hours depart,
love’s gathered fruit outlasts the fading start.

7

In shadowed courts where silent statueth stand,
and marble heroeth watch the years decay,
their frozen brows command the passing land,
yet hear nay voice that clepes them back to day. 
the ivy climbs in slow, encircling grace,
and claims the stoneth once polished bright withal pride,
nay trumpet stirs within that solemn place,
nor banners in triumphant winds abide. 
thus glory built on conquest and on fear
shall crumble like the dust it hoped to reign,
for mortal praise, though loud upon the ear,
cannot preserve what time shall not sustain. 
but virtue, sown in humble, living deed,
outlasts the sculptor’s art and tyrant’s creed.

8

When midnight draws her sable curtain tight,
and lamps grow faint against the swelling gloom,
the mind awakens to its inward sight,
and wanders freely through imagined room. 
unfettered thought ascends on unseen wings,
beyond the bounds o' iron circumstance,
and fashions worlds from small, forgotten things,
granting the soul a sovereign expanse. 
so dreams, though fragile as the morning mist,
reveal desireth daylight dareth not show,
they trace the shapeth by hidden longings kissed,
and plant the seeds from which our actures grow. 
what first in shadowed sleep begins to rise
may bloom in sooth beneath awakened skies.

9

The river moveth withal unresisting will,
and carveth its patient path through rock and clay,
nay storm can force it wholly to be still,
nor drought persuade its current not to stray. 
it bends but breaks not at the mountain’s side,
and finds through yielding strength a forward way,
though cliffs in stubborn opposition bide,
it sings its course from dawn to closing day. 
so let mine own heart in trial learn that art,
to flow around the hardness fate may send,
and not in brittle defiance part,
where wiser softness might the struggle mend. 
for power lieth not still in the blow,
but in the grace by which we onward go.

10

If sorrow press withal unrelenting hand,
and clouds obscure the promise o' the sun,
remember how the smallest grains o' sand
compose the shore where mighty seas are run. 
nay condolement so vast but time shall wear it down,
as waveth smooth jagged stone to gentle form,
nor night so deep it claims the lasting crown,
for dawn returns though long abrook'd the storm. 
hold fast to hope though trembling be thy breath,
and trust the quiet labor o' the years,
for life persists beyond the threat o' death,
and drieth at last the bitterest o' tears. 
what seems an end in anguish sharply drawn
may prove the gate through which new strength is born.

11

When autumn’s breath upon the forest falls,
and crimson crowns the once-emerald trees,
a golden hush along the valley calls,
and bends the wheat in slow, consenting seas. 
the orchards bow withal sweet and ripened weight,
while daylight shortens like a measured sigh,
the sun descends in contemplative state,
and paints its farewell softly on the sky. 
so too the heart that once in summer burned,
must learn to glow withal quieter, deeper flame,
for passions fierce to tempered warmth are turned,
yet lose not strength though altered be their name. 
in mellow light a richer sooth is shown:
what fadeth in fire in embers may be grown.

12

The moon ascends upon a argentine thread,
and batheth the restless town in borrowed gleam,
soft shadows where the forweary labor tread
are gentled by her pale, forgiving beam. 
the rooftops shimmer like a muted tide,
and windows glow as stars in ordered line,
while secrets in the silent streets abide,
untroubled by the clang o' noon’s design. 
thus night reveals what daylight shall not show,
the tender doubts that hide in private breast,
and thoughts that in the crowd refuse to grow,
yet bloom in dark when souls are put to rest. 
in quiet hours the truest voiceth rise,
unmasked beneath the watch o' patient skies.

13

A tempest gathers on the distant plain,
and bends the grasseth base ere its might,
the thunder speaks in resonant refrain,
and cleaveth the trembling firmament withal light. 
the rain descends in fierce, unyielding streams,
and floods the paths where dust had long been king,
yet from the storm awaken buried dreams,
and thirsty roots drink deep from suffering. 
so trials, though they shake the steadfast frame,
may cleanse the heart o' idle vanity,
and through their roar refine the inner flame,
till strength is forged in chosen constancy. 
what storms unmake in pride and shallow art,
they build in courage at the spirit’s heart.

14

The candle burns withal steady, modest glow,
and spends itself to guard the dark at bay,
its wax descends in quiet ebb and flow,
while fragile light holds watch till break o' day. 
nay trumpet marks its sacrificial part,
nor crowd attends its vigil through the night,
yet faithful flame performs a noble art,
in small persistence lieth its truest might. 
so let mine own deeds, though humble in their span,
cast warmth upon the paths where others tread,
for greatness dwells not still in the plan,
but in the gentle word and daily bread. 
a life that giveth though unobserved by fame
may shine more bright than any lauded name.

15

When envy’s seed within the breast is sown,
it sprouts in shadow, pale and twisting fast,
and choketh the joy that might have freely grown,
by clinging fierce to grievanceth long past. 
it measureth worth by others’ passing gain,
and drinks from wells that ne'er satisfy,
till bitterness becometh a tightening chain,
and dims the hopeful prospect o' the eye. 
yet gratitude uproots that creeping vine,
and clears the soil where peace may flourish fair,
it turns the gaze from borrowed, false design,
to gifts already resting in one’s care. 
contented hearts need not another’s throne,
for richeth bloom where thankful seeds are sown.

16

The scholar bends above his weathered page,
and traceth lineth by distant sageth writ,
he seeks to bind within a narrow cage
the boundless truths that time refuseth fit. 
yet knowledge grows not solely from the pen,
nor wisdom dwell in ink alone confined,
for living breath and fellowship withal men
refine the rough conclusions o' the mind. 
let study join withal kindness hand in hand,
and learning serve the common, mortal need,
for sterile thought like root in barren sand
bears little fruit though planted withal a creed. 
true insight flowers where compassion leads,
and thought is wed to generous deeds.

17

The mountain stands against the shifting air,
unmoved by whispers o' the passing breeze,
its ancient face inscribed withal patient care,
a monument to silent history. 
the snows that crown its steadfast, rugged height
melt slowly in the warmth o' summer’s ray,
yet still it keeps its posture in the light,
though ageth grind its granite boneth away. 
so let mine own purpose, firm though weather worn,
abrook the doubts that buffet from below,
for constancy through countless trials borne
outlasts the fervor o' a fleeting show. 
though time erode the surface o' resolve,
a rooted shall nay tempest can dissolve.

18

The lark ascends upon the morning air,
and spills its canton across the waking field,
nay rival voice disputeth its presence thither,
nor hidden bargain shapeth what it must yield. 
it sings because its breast is filled withal tone,
and joy compels the music to be freed,
unmindful whether praise be e'er shown,
or whether any heed its simple creed. 
thus art is honest when born o' inward fire,
and not from thirst for fleeting human gaze,
for hearts respond to honest, clear desire,
more than to skill adorned withal studied praise. 
let canton arise where sooth and gladness meet,
and find in humble flight its meaning sweet.

19

When memory stirs the asheth o' the past,
and paints withal longing sceneth long overthrown,
the mind may wander where its shadows cast
a tender glow on joys nay longer known. 
yet time demands we dwell not wholly thither,
nor build our home in yesterday’s embrace,
for life unfolds in present, breathing air,
and clepes us forth to meet its living face. 
let recollection serve as guiding star,
not anchor dragging hope beneath the wave,
for though it shineth from distance faint and far,
'twas not meant the present to enslave. 
in balanced gaze both past and now agree,
to shape a wiser, forward constancy.

20

If love be fire that neither fadeth nor sleeps,
it warms the coldest chambers o' the soul,
through darkest nights its faithful vigil keeps,
and binds the fractured heart to wholeness whole. 
it doth not boast in loud, triumphant cry,
nor seek applause from fickle, passing throng,
but proveth its strength when trials draweth nigh,
and stands unshaken when the world goeth wrong. 
through changing years and fortune’s altered face,
its steady pulse nay circumstance can sever,
for rooted deep in sacrificial grace,
it weathers all and shall abrook forever. 
let lesser flameth in restless winds depart,
true love burns constant at the center heart.

21

When frost adorns the silent windowpane,
and etcheth lace where summer once had shone,
the hearth within defieth the outer rain,
and claims a warmth the acerb winds disown. 
the logs in measured crackling discourse speak,
and cast their amber counsel on the wall,
while shadows shift in patterns faint and meek,
responsive to the fire’s persuasive call. 
so in adversity the inward spark
may blaze more bright when tempests rage without,
for hardship frameth the light against the dark,
and steels the shall against encroaching doubt. 
what cold would'st quench in careless, softer days,
grows strong when tried by winter’s testing blaze.

22

The market hums beneath the noon’s command,
withal clamor sharp as coin on stone is thrown,
ambition walks withal ledger close at hand,
and weighs each breath in profits yet unknown. 
the banners flap above the restless street,
and promiseth like gaudy wareth are cried,
while hied feet in anxious rhythm beat,
and fortuneth turn withal e'ry shifting tide. 
yet value dwells not solely in the scale,
nor worth confined to what the purse may claim,
for quiet gifts that ne'er stand for sale
outlast the glitter o' a purchased name. 
let purchase be guided by a nobler art,
and commerce tempered by a mindful heart.

23

A child pursueth the echo o' his laugh,
untroubled by the boundarieth o' the hour,
he builds a kingdom from a broken staff,
and crowns himself withal leaveth in budding bower. 
nay fear o' failure dims his eager stride,
nor memory o' loss restrains his play,
imagination stands at his side,
and turns the smallest stone to sovereign sway. 
would'st that the grown might keep such daring sight,
and not exchange bold wonder for control,
for in that fearless trust resideth a light
that guards the freshness o' the aging soul. 
let wisdom join but ne'er quite replace
the courage born o' childhood’s open face.

24

The clock advanceth withal impartial hand,
unmoved by plea or passionate protest,
its steady tick across the ordered land
declareth nay hour more favored than the quietus. 
it grants alike to tyrant and to sage
the measured sum o' moments in a day,
and writeth upon each mortal’s fleeting page
a total none may bargain to delay. 
so spend thy moiety withal deliberate care,
and squander not on trifleth poorly weighed,
for what is lost nay gold can then repair,
nor pleading word persuade what must be paid. 
in mindful use o' e'ry passing breath
resideth the art that conquers wasting death.

25

The rose unfolds beneath the patient sun,
and yields its fragrance freely to the air,
nay audience required nor contest won,
its beauty thriveth though none its bloom declare. 
the bee attends withal base industrious hymn,
and gathers sweetness for another’s need,
while petals at the edge grow faint and dim,
resigned to time’s unhurried quiet speed. 
thus service done without a watchful eye
contains a dignity nay praise can grant,
for deeds that bloom though recognition die
are rooted deep where shallow honors can’t. 
let virtue flower though nay herald sing,
for worth resideth within the offered thing.

26

When rumor spreads on eager careless tongue,
it gathers weight withal each untested claim,
and like a spark through brittle grasseth flung
confounds a field withal unexamined flame. 
the whispered word becometh a sharpened blade,
that wounds unseen yet leaveth a lasting scar,
till trust in cautious silence must be laid,
and friendships wander distant as a star. 
let speech be bridled by discerning mind,
and sooth examined ere it be proclaimed,
for impetuous talk leaveth consequence behind,
and innocent nameth unjustly shamed. 
in tempered words lieth honor’s quiet guard,
and peace preserved where slander would'st strike hard.

27

The traveler sets his course by distant light,
though mileth o' shadow stretch betwixt his feet,
he trusts the faint but faithful gleam in sight,
and counts each forweary step a small defeat. 
 the road may twist through valleys steep and bare,
and hunger press where comfort once wast known,
yet hope persists in thin and bracing air,
and strengthens hearts long tested and alone. 
 so let the goal though distant as the dawn
direct the stride when present ground is rough,
for many noble ends are slowly drawn,
and worth the cost though passage prove enough. 
 by steady gaze on promise yet unseen
the far horizon narrows in betweeen.

28

The painter lifts his brush withal thoughtful pause,
and studieth light that shifts upon the wall,
he wots that nature yields to hidden laws,
yet seeks to capture more than what befall. 
a stroke too bold may mar the tender hue,
a timid line leave vision incomplete,
thus censure guideth what skill alone can do,
and patient hands pursue a balance meet. 
so craft in any noble human art
requireth a marriage o' the heart and mind,
for passion without measure falls apart,
and reason cold leaveth living fire behind. 
where discipline and ardor intertwine,
the work achieveth a harmony divine.

29

When doubt invadeth withal subtle creeping tread,
and questions whisper what the heart once knew,
it paints withal ashen shadeth what hope had spread,
and dims the vibrant promise once in view. 
yet scrutiny though sharp in searching aim
may cleanse belief o' careless idle trust,
and purge from faith the dross o' hollow claim,
till what remains is gold refined from dust. 
let doubt not reign as tyrant o' the mind,
nor abandon all conviction from its seat,
but serve as steward stern yet still confined,
that sooth more sound and tested may be meet. 
in balanced thought where challenge shapeth belief,
the spirit finds both firmness and relief.

30

The harbor attends withal arms o' curving stone,
and gathers ships returned from storm and spray,
their battered sails and weathered hulls are shown,
yet safe at last within the sheltered bay. 
the ropeth are cast and forweary crews descend,
withal taleth o' peril borne on salted breath,
each voyage marked by loss and faithful friend,
and narrow passage close to grasp o' death. 
so home becometh the anchor o' the soul,
a steadfast quay against the tossing sea,
where fractured parts are gently made more whole,
and restless hearts regain their constancy. 
in welcome warmth where wandering journeys cease,
the tempest’s roar giveth way to healing peace.

31

The lantern swings against the evening breeze,
and casts its circle on the cobbled way,
a modest star among the darkened trees,
that guards the traveler till the break o' day. 
though small its flame against the vault o' night,
it doth not waver from its chosen ground,
but keeps a faithful watch withal steady light,
where larger fireth are nowhere to be found. 
so let mine own words, though quiet in their sound,
give guidance where confusion veils the air,
for sooth need not in thunder be unbound,
to serve as compass in a world o' care. 
a single spark that holds against the gale
may prove more sure than torcheth vast and pale.

32

The sculptor studieth marble cold and bare,
and seeth within what others fail to trace,
a hidden curve, a form both strong and fair,
awaiting patient hands to grant it face. 
each strike removeth what doth not yet belong,
each measured blow refineth the inward shape,
till from the block emergeth clear and strong
a figure freed from stone’s enclosing drape. 
so too the self by discipline is formed,
as flaws are chipped and roughness pared away,
for through resistance character is warmed,
and purpose carved from common clay. 
by patient cuts the truest lineth appear,
and what wast trapped stands radiant and clear.

33

When whispers drift across the midnight hall,
and ancient rafters creak in solemn tone,
the house recalls the footsteps once in thrall,
o' liveth long lived and storieth overthrown. 
the portraits fade yet still retain a gaze,
as though their silent watch would'st not depart,
and shadows move in long remembered ways,
that stir the chambers o' a listening heart. 
so memory inhabits present space,
and lingers where affection once wast laid,
it leaveth upon the air a subtle trace,
though years have dimmed what time hath not delayed. 
in quiet rooms where past and present meet,
the pulse o' vanished days continueth beat.

34
The ploughman turns the furrowed field at dawn,
And cleaveth earth with steady, measured stride,
No herald greets the labour he hath drawn,
Nor crowds attend to watch the soil divide.
Yet from his toil the hidden seed is cast,
And cover'd by the patient, yielding ground,
Though rain may fall and bitter winds blow past,
In silent dark its promise shall be found.
So work unseen may bear the richest yield,
Though applause be absent from its faithful art,
For fruit doth ripen in the humble field,
Long after idle boast hath lost its start.
What patient hands commit to careful earth
Shall rise in time with undeniable worth.

35

The distant bell upon the harbour tower
Announceth hours the tide cannot ignore,
Its measured toll doth mark all fleeting power,
Against the rhythm of the ocean's roar.
The ships may come and go at fortune's will,
Their sails inflated by uncertain breeze,
Yet steadfast stands the tower on the hill,
Unmov'd by trade or tempests of the seas.
Thus principle must anchor wand'ring mind,
And hold its course when currents twist and pull,
For shifting winds may leave the soul behind,
If guided only by what seemeth full.
In fixed conviction wisely held and known,
The heart withstandeth what change hath o'erthrown.

36

When rivalry igniteth the restless heart,
And stirreth ambition's bright but dangerous flame,
It urgeth men their fellows to outstart,
And measure worth by triumph and acclaim.
Yet contest need not poison noble aim,
If temper'd well by fairness and respect,
For striving sharpen'd free from scornful blame
May lift both sides toward excellence direct.
Let victory be crown'd with gracious speech,
And loss receiv'd without resentful art,
For honour stands within the way we reach
The goal that testeth sinew and the heart.
In contest shap'd by virtue's guiding hand,
Both gain a strength no gold can command.

37

The rain descendeth on the city stone,
And draweth from the pavement scents of spring,
It writeth silver script on streets alone,
A transient yet most expressive off'ring.
The hurried crowd beneath their umbrellas pass,
Unmindful of the cleansing gift above,
Yet every drop upon the thirsting grass
Prepareth earth for roots of future love.
So blessings fall unnotic'd in their hour,
And seem but inconvenience to the eye,
Till later blooms reveal their quiet power,
And gratitude replaceth former sigh.
What first appeareth burden or delay
May nourish hidden gardens on the way.

38

The minstrel tuneth his lute before the crowd,
And testeth each string with careful, list'ning ear,
He will not trust to volume bold and loud,
But seeketh notes that ring both true and clear.
For harmony dependeth on balanc'd strain,
Too tight the cord may snap beneath its might,
Too loose it yields a dull, uncertain pain,
And faileth to carry melody aright.
So lives must find their tension rightly set,
'Twixt indulgence and austerity,
For either bound too firmly met
Distorteth the song of full humanity.
In temper'd stretch where measur'd forces blend,
The sweetest tones upon the air ascend.

39

The pilgrim kneeleth by the winding road,
And resteth his staff against the ancient stone,
His shoulders bear a long and dusty load,
Yet hope within his breast remaineth his own.
No promise written on the sky is shown,
Nor sudden voice descendeth to clear his fear,
Yet faith sustaineth what reason leaves alone,
And guideth feet though destination unclear.
Thus trust may flourish where no sign appears,
And strength arise without external proof,
For inward certainty through passing years
Becometh shelter of the seeking roof.
In steadfast steps though sight be dim or far,
The heart proceedeth by its end'ring star.

40

When twilight yields at last to breaking dawn,
And pale horizon kindleth into flame,
The night that seem'd eternal now is gone,
And fear receedeth that once held its claim.
The birds resume their bright and certain song,
And dew adorneth the meadow's waiting face,
While light restoreth what the darkness hid so long,
And breatheth new vigour into silent space.
So sorrow too shall fade before the day,
However vast its shadow seem'd at first,
For grief like night must yield to rising ray,
And hope returneth to quench the spirit's thirst.
No darkness holds dominion without end,
Where faithful light prepareth to ascend.

41

When silver rain upon the rooftop falls,
And murmureth low against the shuttered pane,
It threadeth through the night in soft, unhurried calls,
And cooleth the fev'red memory of pain.
The streets grow dim beneath its gentle sweep,
And lantern light grows blurr'd in amber haze,
While weary minds descend to needed sleep,
Releas'd from daylight's sharp and crowded maze.
So mercy comes not always crown'd in might,
But sometimes cloth'd in quiet, steady grace,
It dims the edge of anger's sharpen'd sight,
And smootheth the furrow'd lines upon the face.
In patient drops that ask no grand display,
The harshest heat is slowly wash'd away.

42

The captain stands before the rising gale,
His weather'd hand upon the creaking mast,
He knoweth well the canvas yet may fail,
And fragile wood may splinter in the blast.
Yet fear shall not unman his settled gaze,
Nor trembling doubt command his chosen course,
For courage grows in trial's testing blaze,
And steadieth will against opposing force.
So leadership is prov'd in storm, not sun,
When easy tides no skilful art require,
And honour shines in what is firmly done,
Though all around seems bent on dark desire.
The helm held fast through tempests fierce and wild
Secureth safe harbour for the weakest child.

43

A single seed within the barren ground
Awaiteth the kiss of rain and warming light,
No sign of life upon its husk is found,
Yet hidden strength prepareth for upward fight.
The soil above appeareth a heavy tomb,
Unyielding weight against its fragile core,
Yet from that press shall living green resume,
And split the earth that seal'd it days before.
So buried hopes beneath discouragement
May gather force unseen by outward eye,
Till sudden growth in resolute ascent
Breaks through the doubt that bade ambition die.
What seems entomb'd in silence and despair
May rise renew'd in fresher, braver air.

44

The scholar's lamp burns late against the dark,
And scattereth gold across the waiting page,
He searcheth truth in every subtle mark,
And weigheth each word with disciplin'd engage.
Yet wisdom dwells not solely in the text,
Nor knowledge rest in volumes tightly bound,
For liv'd experience stands close annexed,
And deepeneth thought where ink alone is found.
Let study join with humble, patient deed,
And theory meet the temp'ring of life,
For seeds of thought require embodied seed,
To stand unshaken amid worldly strife.
When mind and action walk in measur'd pace,
Understanding findeth its truest place.

45

The meadow wakes beneath the tender dawn,
And lifts its dewy lashes to the sun,
The hush of night in quiet folds withdrawn,
As golden light declareth the day begun.
The lark ascendeth with fearless, ringing cry,
And spills its anthem over field and stream,
While shadows shrink before the warming sky,
And earth emergeth from its fading dream.
So hope returneth though grief hath held its reign,
And lifts the heart from sorrow's heavy shroud,
For morning writeth light across our pain,
A promise clear though yesterday was loud.
No night persists beyond appointed span,
Where patient dawn reneweth the strength of man.

46

The mason sets each stone with careful hand,
And checks the line against the guiding thread,
He builds not haste but structure meant to stand,
Long after fleeting praise hath long since fled.
Each layer rests upon the one below,
And bears the weight of what is yet to rise,
For foundations hidden none may show
Determine if the tallest tower flies.
So character is form'd in unseen hours,
In habits small and choices little prais'd,
For public triumph draws from secret powers,
In private disciplines securely rais'd.
What stands secure against the wind and rain
Was fashion'd first in effort free of gain.

47

The tide retreats along the gleaming shore,
And leaves behind a map of shining sand,
The shells once toss'd in ocean's vibrant roar
Now rest in quiet reach of searching hand.
The waves return in measur'd, ancient art,
And claim again what briefly lay reveal'd,
Thus loss and gain perform their shifting part,
As fortunes rise and fall in turning field.
Let not the heart grow proud in rising swell,
Nor faint when waters seem to draw away,
For rhythms greater than our will compel
The ebb and flow that mark each mortal day.
In patient trust through change that none can bar,
We learn to stand where constant cycles are.

48

The gard'ner trims the branch that grows astray,
And prunes excess that chokes the tender vine,
He sacrificeth bloom of present day,
To shape a harvest richer in design.
The cut may seem a loss in passing glance,
And leaves appear diminish'd to the eye,
Yet future fruit dependeth on measur'd chance,
And present loss prepareth abundance nigh.

49

51

52

53

55

59

60

63

64

65

66

67

68

69

70

The trav'ler nearing twilight of his road

71

78

79

80

82

For patient force though gentle in its way
Can shape the earth beneath the changing sky.
So influence through quiet, constant deed
Transformeth ground beyond its modest start,
For kindness planted as a humble seed
May alter landscapes of the human heart.
In silent stream where faithful currents run,
The strongest change through soft persistence won.

199

The builder drawing lines on measur'd ground
Envisioneth arches rising toward the air,
He knows that form before in thought is found
Must live in careful labour and repair.
No tower stands that first was not conceiv'd
Within the disciplin'd and order'd mind,
For what in vision firmly is believ'd
May later in embodi'd strength be sign'd.
Thus thought precedeth the shaping of the deed,
And imagination cleareth the path,
For seeds of change begin in inward seed
Before they bloom in consequence and aftermath.
In careful plans where bold designs commence,
The outer world reflecteth the inward sense.

200

When twilight folds the sunlight from the land,
And constellations climb the silent dome,
The world grows still beneath a gentler hand,
And wand'ring thought returneth last to home.
The labours done, the reckonings complete,
The spirit turns to weigh what hath been sown,
For quiet dusk inviteth reflection sweet
On seeds of word and deed that have been thrown.
So endings serve not merely as decline,
But portals where renewal may begin,
For cycles trace their ever-turning line,
And calm restoreth what strain had press'd within.
In restful hush where striving comes to cease,
The soul reneweth its strength in peace.
